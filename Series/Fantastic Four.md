# Fantastic Four

I have Epic 1 and read it. I liked it, from what I remember.

## Why I read it
Fantastic Four is Marvel's first family and the start of the Silver Age. It was suggested as a good fit for your taste because Reed Richards is the closest thing to Tony Stark's tech-genius side, and Doctor Doom (Reed's enemy, and Iron Man's and Strange's) ties it to your top three.

## Plan and status
- **Parked line, not in the rotation yet.** You own and have read Epic 1. Nothing else is planned or bought; this page lists the whole line so you can see what it would take.
- **Where the books are.** The Epic Collections 1 to 14 cover Fantastic Four #1 to #264 (1961 to 1983). **Epics 15 and 16 (#265 to #295, the end of Byrne's run) are not released**, the same kind of gap as Iron Man's Epic 9; the Byrne omnibus volumes cover that stretch. Epics 17 to 26 cover 1986 to 1997.
- **Already known:** Epic 2 was seen at 125 on Cheap-Comics (too expensive), Epic 3 is also in the vault, and there is a Fantastic Four Omnibus Vol 2 (#31 to #60) as an alternative to Epics 2 and 3. No other prices are known.
- **What the two reading-order sites say:** comicbookreadingorders.com puts Epics 1 to 3 and the Lee and Kirby omnibus 2 on its route, then jumps to later runs. The Comic Book Herald lists the Lee and Kirby omnibuses (Vol 1 #1 to #30, Vol 2 #31 to #60, Vol 3 #61 to #93), then the Masterworks Vol 10 to 18 (#94 to #203), the Byrne omnibuses (#215 to #295 and extras) and Epic Collections from *The More Things Change* (#308 to #320) onward, then Waid and Wieringo and later runs.
- **If you want it as a line,** the natural start is Epic 2, then Epic 3 (the Galactus story), at about one unit per round in the [[Reading Rotation]]. That would be a sixth line, so I would add it only after the Iron Man, Strange and Thor books are in hand.
